# Beverage Vendor Performance Analysis

## Executive Summary

Your top 10 vendors represent **65% of total revenue** (~$294M), with several concerning patterns that require immediate attention.

---

## Concentration Risk

**Moderate-to-High Concern**

- **Diageo alone commands 15.2%** of your revenue—if they face supply disruptions or change terms, you're significantly exposed
- Your **top 3 vendors control 31.5%** of revenue (Diageo, Martignetti, Pernod Ricard)
- The flip side: you have reasonable diversification across the remaining vendors, with no other single vendor exceeding 7.2%

---

## Margin Concerns

**Critical Issues Identified**

| Vendor | Revenue | Margin | Problem |
|--------|---------|--------|---------|
| **Martignetti** | $41M | **-37.1%** | Losing ~$15M annually |
| **M S Walker** | $15.4M | **-33.3%** | Losing ~$5M annually |
| **Ultra Beverage** | $17.8M | **-17.4%** | Losing ~$3M annually |

These three vendors represent **$74M in revenue but are destroying profitability.** You're essentially paying them to take your business.

**Underperformers:**
- Constellation Brands (10.1%) and E&J Gallo (11.4%) deliver weak margins despite significant volume

**Bright Spot:**
- Bacardi delivers your **best margin at 33.4%** with modest revenue—potential growth opportunity

---

## Top 3 Recommendations

### 1. Immediately Renegotiate or Exit Negative-Margin Relationships
Martignetti, M S Walker, and Ultra Beverage are costing you approximately **$23M in annual losses**. Demand pricing corrections within 30 days or begin transition planning. No volume justifies losing money on every sale.

### 2. Rebalance Toward High-Margin Vendors
Shift volume toward Bacardi (33.4%), Pernod Ricard (25.8%), and Diageo (22.4%). Even a 10% volume shift from loss-makers to these vendors could recover millions in margin.

### 3. Reduce Diageo Dependency
While Diageo performs well on margin, 15.2% concentration is risky. Develop secondary suppliers in their key categories to create negotiating leverage and supply chain resilience.

---

*Bottom line: You have a margin crisis masquerading as a vendor management problem. Fix the three money-losing relationships first—everything else is secondary.*
